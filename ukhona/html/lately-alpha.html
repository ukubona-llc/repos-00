Three Moves That Make You Cry
→
The Deceptive Glide
ii7 to iii7 · Diatonic Safety

The walk-up opens with a completely safe, diatonic move: E♭m7 to Fm7. Bass ascends a whole step. Chord quality stays the same (minor 7th to minor 7th). The ear registers this as comfortable, predictable forward motion — you feel securely anchored in D♭ major. Stevie is building trust so he can betray it.

⚡
The Heartbreak
iii7 to iv7 · Modal Interchange

Here is the twist. Instead of rising a whole step to the bright, expected IVmaj7 (G♭maj7), Stevie slides the same minor-7th shape up only a half step to G♭m7. This is the iv minor — borrowed from D♭ minor. The B♭♭ (enharmonic A) doesn't exist in D♭ major. It arrives on the lyric "start to cry." The dark cloud. The knife.

↓
The Exhausted Sigh
V7(♭9) to Imaj7 · Altered Dominant Release

To get home, he moves to A♭7 — but colors it with a ♭9 (B♭♭/A natural), another tone native to the minor scale. By stacking minor-key tensions over the dominant, the resolution to D♭maj7 feels not like triumph but like an exhausted, beautiful surrender. You have been crying. Now you breathe.
