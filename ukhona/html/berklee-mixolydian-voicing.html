Why omit the root from the right hand? Because the root is a ground. When you remove the ground from the upper register, the tensions have nothing to resolve against locally — they float. The bass provides the anchor; the right hand provides the cloud above it. That is the D'Angelo sound: anchored in the body, weightless above the shoulders.
III · The Phrygian Connection · Spanish Flamenco · Jazzed Into Neo-Soul
PHRYGIAN DOMINANT

The Phrygian Connection

Phrygian Dominant · Spanish Phrygian · Flamenco Guitarist · Jazz Function · Neo-Soul "Stink"

The ♭9 and ♭13 are the defining characteristics of the Phrygian mode — the mode built on the second degree of a major scale, or equivalently, the mode that uses a half-step above the tonic as its characteristic color. In its "Dominant" form (Phrygian Dominant = 5th mode of harmonic minor), it produces the sound universally recognized as flamenco: a dominant chord with a flat-nine that doesn't quite resolve, that circles the tonic and withdraws.

By placing the ♭9 and ♭13 over a jazz dominant seventh chord, the voicing is "jazzing up" the sound of a flamenco guitarist. The Spanish Phrygian is not borrowed or exotic in this context — it is the underlying grammar. The ♯9 (which is absent from pure Phrygian) is the jazz addition: the chromatic rub that makes the chord simultaneously ancient (Phrygian) and modern (altered dominant).

The Calculus Analogy · D'Angelo's V7 as a Limit
$$\lim_{t \to \text{resolution}} V7(\flat9,\sharp9,\flat13) = I_{\text{maj7}} \quad \text{but } t \text{ never arrives}$$

In "Untitled (How Does It Feel)," the V7 approaches resolution the way a limit approaches its value — arbitrarily close, never touching. The function is defined everywhere except at the resolution point itself. The yearning is the mathematics. The "stagnant tension" Vibe described is not a stall — it is an infinite approach. The destination exists. It is simply never occupied.

This is also why "Untitled" creates a hypnotic, almost stagnant tension. A V7 that resolves creates forward motion. A V7 that refuses to resolve becomes a standing wave — it vibrates in place, producing the same harmonic energy repeatedly without dissipating it. The groove locks in because the harmonic tension never drops. The listener's body responds to the groove while their harmonic expectation remains perpetually suspended. That is the neo-soul mechanism: groove satisfies the body; unresolved harmony keeps the mind searching.

The ♭9 and ♭13 are Phrygian. The ♯9 is jazz. Together they produce a dominant chord that feels simultaneously ancient and modern, Spanish and American, flamenco and funk. It is the oldest musical tension in Western tradition, rerouted through Detroit and Virginia Beach.
IV · Daily Practice · The "Stretched" Circle · I – ii – V(alt) – I
THE STRETCHED CIRCLE

Daily Practice: The "Stretched" Circle

Replace every V7 in the cheat sheet with this altered voicing · four-step loop · the "Big Sigh"

Take the Diatonic Seventh Cheat Sheet and replace every V7 chord with this altered voicing. Practice the following four-step loop in every key, in Circle of Fifths order. The loop is short on purpose: the point is to experience the altered dominant's resolution — the "Big Sigh" — repeatedly, until the tension and release become motor memory rather than conscious calculation.

in C: Cmaj7 → Dm7 → G7(♭9,♯9,♭13) → Cmaj7 ← Big Sigh
1.
Play Imaj7 HOME
The tonic — your base of operations. Feel its stability completely before you move. In C: Cmaj7 (C–E–G–B). Hold it long enough to establish "home" as a felt sense, not just a concept.
2.
Play ii−7 PRE-TENSION
The pre-dominant. This is where the harmonic motion begins — a gentle lean away from the tonic. In C: Dm7 (D–F–A–C). It introduces mild tension without committing to anything dramatic. Think of it as the question mark before the exclamation point.
3.
Play V7(♭9, ♯9, ♭13) THE D'ANGELO MOMENT
The altered dominant — the entire machinery described above. In C: G7(♭9,♯9,♭13). Left hand: G and F. Right hand: B – E♭ – A♭ – B♭. This is maximum harmonic entropy. Every tension the altered scale can offer, compressed into one chord. Sit in it. Let the stink settle. Don't rush to step 4.
4.
Resolve to Imaj7 THE BIG SIGH
The return. After the altered dominant's accumulated tension, the Imaj7 feels like physical relief — an exhale after held breath. In C: back to Cmaj7. This is the "Big Sigh." With enough practice, the sigh becomes automatic: your body will relax at the resolution before your mind registers what happened.

Practice this loop in all 12 keys, Circle of Fifths order. When the altered dominant voicing is fully under your fingers, extend the loop: delay the resolution. Play step 3 twice. Three times. Sustain the G7(♭9,♯9,♭13) until the tension becomes a groove, not a discomfort. That is the D'Angelo move — not "I can't find the resolution" but "I don't need it yet." The yearning is the music.

The "Stretched" Circle is a machine for building tolerance of harmonic entropy. The longer you can sustain the altered dominant before resolving, the more control you have over the listener's body. D'Angelo's genius is that he stays in step 3 for minutes at a time — and the listener follows willingly, because the groove makes the tension feel like home.

Synthesis · The D'Angelo Voicing · What It All Means

The Secret Sauce Is Not a Secret — It Is a Commitment

The altered dominant is not a difficult chord. It is a commitment. A commitment to staying in maximum harmonic tension longer than is comfortable, longer than is safe, long enough that the listener's body stops waiting for the resolution and begins to inhabit the tension as if it were its own. That is what D'Angelo does in "Untitled." He does not delay the resolution as a compositional technique. He delays it until the listener forgets they were waiting — and then, when it comes, the sigh is larger than any resolution earned through conventional means could be.

The Phrygian connection is real. The Spanish Phrygian has been doing this for centuries in flamenco — building tension to the edge of unbearability and then refusing to cross it. Neo-soul is flamenco with a Fender Rhodes and a drum machine. The grammar is ancient. The vocabulary is American. The "stink" is the smell of two traditions recognizing each other across the octave.

The calculus analogy holds: the limit exists. The function approaches it. The value is never achieved. In mathematics this is a feature, not a failure. In music it is the same. The V7(♭9,♯9,♭13) is a function defined everywhere except at its own resolution. The yearning is the definition. The "Untitled" track is a five-minute proof that the most beautiful value a function can have is the one it never quite reaches.
