The formula encodes a profound structural fact: the twelve-tone scale is a geometric sequence, not an arithmetic one. Equal temperament means each semitone is the same ratio, not the same distance. This is why the piano is a fractal instrument — the same chord shape, played anywhere on the keyboard, preserves all internal ratios. The intervals are scale-invariant. The geometry is self-similar. Every key is the same as every other key, rotated.

This is also why practicing in all 12 keys is not repetition. It is the traversal of a geometric orbit. You are not playing the same thing in a different place. You are visiting the same shape at a different frequency — and discovering what your hands and ears know about each position in the orbit.

A440 is not the origin. It is the arbitrary anchor point of an infinite recursive structure. The chords you practice today are not the content. They are the sampling of a function defined for all n.

III · The Wildcard Strategy · Pivot Practice · Wormhole Exercise
The Wormhole Exercise · Stevie Wonder Level Mastery

Once the 12-key table is under your fingers, the "Stevie Wonder" level comes from Pivot Practice — the ability to re-visualize any chord as a different function in a different key, in real time, without stopping.

1.
Pick a iv−7 chord. For example: G♭m7 in the key of D♭. You know this chord — it's the iv−7. You've just played it in the table above, in D♭'s row.
D♭ Major → G♭m7 (iv−7)
2.
Immediately re-visualize. That same G♭m7 chord — same notes, same voicing — is also F♯m7 (enharmonic) and functions as the vi−7 in A Major. Do not change your hand. Change your mental frame.
G♭m7 = F♯m7 ≡ vi−7 in A Major
3.
Resolve into A Major. From your new mental frame, play the I chord — A Major (or Amaj7). You've just navigated the D♭ → A jump with no math. A single re-frame, held for one chord duration.
vi−7 in A → Amaj7 (I)
4.
Continue the chain. From A Major, find the ii−7 (Bm7) and the V7 (E7). Now pivot again: treat E7 as a ♭VII7 in G♭, or treat the whole A Major area as a temporary tonic before entering the G♭ system. This is how you navigate D♭ → A → G♭ in real time — without getting lost in the math.
D♭ ⟿ G♭m7 ⟿ A Major ⟿ G♭ system the full "Lately" bridge in real time
PIVOT LOGIC

Why This Works: The Chord Is Not the Key

Enharmonic re-spelling · functional re-framing · one chord · two (or four) tonal citizenships

The wormhole exercise works because a chord's function is not intrinsic to its notes — it is assigned by the tonal frame you are currently in. G♭m7 and F♯m7 are the same frequencies. They are different functions. Switching the function without changing the notes is a mental operation, not a physical one. This is what "tonal vertigo resistance" means: the ability to hold your physical position (the chord under your hands) while rotating your mental frame around it.

Practice this daily: for each V7 in the table, find its enharmonic partner chord one key away. Treat the V7 as a ♭VII7 in the key a half-step above. Treat the ii−7 as a iv−7 in the key a major third below. Build the habit of double-reading every chord — as its function in the current key, and as a potential pivot into an adjacent one.

SATURATION

Why Both ♭9 and ♯9 Simultaneously?

The Spanish Phrygian converted to jazz-functional tension · two minor seconds at once · the sound of harmonic entropy at maximum

The ♭9 and ♯9 are, enharmonically, a minor second apart (♭9 = one semitone above the root, ♯9 = one semitone above the major 2nd). Playing both simultaneously creates a cluster of chromatic tension in the upper register. This should sound dense. It should feel saturated. That is the point. The "absolute harmonic saturation" is not a bug — it is the acoustic equivalent of the vii°7 opening: a chord that belongs to multiple tonal frames at once, and therefore to none specifically.

The ♭13 (enharmonically: ♯5) adds the Lydian-Dominant shimmer described in the "Lately" bridge analysis. Together, the 3–♭13–♭9–♯9 voicing is the most compact encoding of the maximum tension a dominant chord can express before resolution. Stevie uses this — or functional equivalents — at every structural pivot. It is the sound of the harmonic system at its highest entropy state, one chord before surrender.

Full altered dominant · note degrees · semitone positions from root $$V7_{\text{alt}} = \{R,\ \flat7\} \oplus \{3,\ \flat13,\ \flat9,\ \sharp9\}$$ Left hand: intervals 0 + 10 semitones (root + ♭7)

The result is absolute harmonic saturation. Not because more notes are better, but because these six specific pitch classes are the six that make the dominant chord simultaneously point toward resolution and refuse to commit to it.

Synthesis · The Practice · What It Builds

What the Eternal Recurrence Actually Is

The table is not a reference sheet. It is a daily orbit. You begin at C, and you travel through the Circle of Fifths — adding one sharp, then another, then reaching the enharmonic equator (F♯/G♭), then descending through the flat keys back to C. Each key is the same structure at a different frequency. Each traversal deepens the geometry in your hands and ears until the structure is motor, not cognitive.

The pivot exercise is what turns the orbit into navigation. Once you know that G♭m7 is iv−7 in D♭ and vi−7 in A simultaneously, you can move between keys the way Stevie does — not by modulating, but by re-framing. The chord does not change. The tonal frame rotates around it. This is the "wormhole" described in the "Lately" analysis, practiced as a daily physical habit.

The altered dominant voicing is what turns navigation into expression. The 3–♭13–♭9–♯9 right-hand voicing is not an embellishment of the V7. It is the V7 at maximum harmonic entropy — the state in which it contains, simultaneously, the maximum number of chromatic tensions before it resolves. This is the sound that distinguishes "playing changes" from "playing inside the changes." Absolute harmonic saturation, then release. That is the only formula.
