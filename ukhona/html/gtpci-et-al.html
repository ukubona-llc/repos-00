Trends in ECG Monitoring Usage

There is a substantial uptick in long-term ambulatory ECG monitoring over recent years. One graph shows 3–14 day monitoring jumping from ~0% in 2009 to 6.9% in 2022 after a clinical visit across 34 health providers in the All of Us Research Program. 24–48 hour monitors dipped slightly, while MCT (mobile cardiac telemetry) up to 30 days stayed relatively flat.

Increases are most prominent in patients with SVT (supraventricular tachycardia), driven by those with AF. Usage rose sharply for new users of 3–14 day devices: 13% in 2017–2020 → 39% in 2021–2022.

Device Evolution

Historical: Holter device (1940s, 24–48 hours, electrodes/wires); Implantable loop recorder (1990s, months–years, invasive).

Novel long-term "patch" ECG monitors (Zio Patch/iRhythm/ePatch/BioTel, entered US market 2007–2009): 3–14 days, leadless, water-resistant, simple to use, better compliance, automated first-pass ECG data processing.

Mobile cardiac telemetry (MCT): Entered 2007–2009, up to 30 days with real-time arrhythmia recognition and data transmission — though some models lack continuous recording.

Related Research

A Circulation article on "Atrial Fibrillation Burden and Heart Failure Events From Cardiac Implantable Electronic Devices" (n = 39,710 individuals) uses Medicare claims data 2015–2020 linked to the Medtronic CareLink database. Key finding: Higher AF burden correlates with increased heart failure risk (adjusted HR 1.10 per 10% increase).

Article II

Graduate Training Programs in Clinical Investigation
Johns Hopkins Bloomberg SPH × School of Medicine

The Graduate Training Programs in Clinical Investigation (GTPCI) is a pioneering initiative jointly offered by the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health and the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine. Established as the first program of its kind, it trains clinicians and allied health professionals to become effective clinical scientists — equipping them to design, conduct, and interpret clinical investigations involving human subjects.

History

GTPCI originated from a 1989 Task Force on Clinical Research within the Johns Hopkins Department of Medicine, which identified significant training gaps among clinical post-doctoral fellows. Surveys revealed that about one-third aspired to full-time clinical research careers, but felt underprepared:

  • 70% in clinical trial design and data management
  • 69% in research ethics
  • 83% in biostatistics

This led to GTPCI's creation in 1992, first students admitted 1993. In 2023, four academic pathways were introduced for greater flexibility.

Admissions

Applications due March 1 annually for PhD and MHS.

  • PhD: Must hold a JHMI postdoctoral fellowship or junior faculty role; letter of support confirming 3 years of funding and 80% protected time.
  • MHS: Advanced medical degree required; financial support from organization or government for external applicants.
  • GTPCI encourages completing most clinical training before starting.

Funding: GTPCI does not provide funding. Support typically comes from NIH training grants (KL2, K23), career development awards, or departmental funds.

Article III

Harvard Comparanda:
Three Analogs to GTPCI Across Cambridge & Longwood

While Harvard does not have an exact replica of GTPCI, several programs at Harvard Medical School and the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health offer comparable training in clinical investigation, translational research, evidence-based medicine, epidemiology, and biostatistics.

I
Closest Analog to GTPCI MHS

Master of Medical Sciences in Clinical Investigation (MMSCI)

Duration2 years full-time / 3 years part-time
Credits64
DeadlineMar 31 / Feb 18 (international)
Degree req.MD, PhD, MBBS, or equiv.
Tuition~$60,000/year

A two-year, full-time master's program designed for future leaders in clinical and translational research. Combines didactic coursework with a parallel mentored research experience at a Harvard-affiliated institution in Boston.

Tracks: Clinical Investigation (Comparative Research or Clinical Trials) · Translational Investigation (bench-to-bedside). First-year classes are shared; second-year is customized.

Curriculum: Biostatistics, epidemiology, clinical trial design, grant writing, research ethics, data analysis, scientific communication. Mentored research component culminates in thesis or capstone.

II
Accelerated · Evidence-Based

Master of Science in Clinical Research (MSCR)

Duration1 year full-time
FocusEvidence-based medicine
DeadlineMar 31, 2026
PedagogyFlipped classroom

A one-year, full-time master's program launched to train leaders in evidence-based medicine. Leverages HMS's network and affiliated hospitals, emphasizing high-impact evidence synthesis amid advancements like AI and gene therapy.

Curriculum: Epidemiology, biostatistics, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, decision sciences, guideline development, mixed methods, applied omics. Capstone: systematic review or meta-analysis.

Who applies: Fellows, residents, junior faculty, nurses, social workers, current medical students. Less research-intensive than MMSCI or GTPCI PhD.

III
PhD Level · Population Health

PhD in Population Health Sciences (PHS)

Duration4–5 years full-time
Admits~29/year from 350–850 applicants
DeadlineDec 1 annually
GRERequired
FundingOften full support (stipend + tuition + insurance)

A 4–5 year, full-time, cohort-based PhD under the Harvard Griffin Graduate School of Arts and Sciences. Integrates biological, physical, and social sciences to address large-scale public health issues.

Fields: Environmental Health · Epidemiology (most similar to clinical investigation) · Global Health & Population · Nutrition · Social & Behavioral Sciences.

Curriculum: 18.75 core credits in quantitative methods (regression, causal inference, bias), epidemiology, history/politics of public health, research ethics. Qualifying exams → dissertation proposal → advisory committee → defense.
